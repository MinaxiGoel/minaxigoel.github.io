CLARIVATE · A/B TESTING · VWO · CRO · SESSION RECORDINGS

Improved EndNote Contact Sales form completion through A/B testing

I tested whether breaking a long form into smaller steps could reduce effort and improve lead generation.

The Problem

  • The EndNote Contact Sales form was long, and users were leaving before submitting it.
  • We needed to know if showing fewer fields at a time would improve form completion without hurting clarity.

Research in Action

  • Set up a VWO A/B test: single-step control vs. multi-step variation.
  • Reviewed session recordings to see how users moved through the form, not just whether they converted.

Actionable Insight

  • The multi-step design reduced the visible load, so the form felt shorter.
  • Average completion moved from 3m29s to 1m52s, pointing to lower user effort.

Outcome

  • +12.6% submissions from the multi-step experience.
  • 46.41% faster completion, giving the team a clear case to scale form optimization.

CLARIVATE · VOICE OF CUSTOMER · NPS · KEY DRIVER ANALYSIS · THEMATIC ANALYSIS

Ran the Annual Voice of Customer program to improve NPS and find innovation gaps

I used customer feedback to understand what needed attention and where teams could take more data-driven action.

The Problem

  • Teams needed a clearer customer signal to improve experience, retention, advocacy, and perceived value.
  • Participation needed to grow so teams could trust the data and prioritize the right experience drivers.

Research in Action

  • Measured purchase experience, product quality, functionality, innovation, service, education, and communication.
  • Used QR codes, events, web, social, and email footers to increase responses, then ran key-driver and thematic analysis.

Actionable Insight

  • Communication, innovation, functionality, and product quality were important to customers but underperforming.
  • The findings gave teams a practical list of areas to improve and detractors to close the loop with.

Outcome

  • NPS increased by 3 points in 2025.
  • Responses increased by 24.7% to 19,667, with response rate reaching 2%.

MAGICBRICKS · RAG LLM · HUGGING FACE · GOOGLE COLAB · DOCUMENT AI

RERA document chatbot POC

I created a working Python-based RAG prototype so users could ask questions against messy RERA documents instead of reading everything manually.

The Problem

  • Property buyers had to search hundreds of RERA documents to find project-specific answers.
  • Legal details, builder reputation signals, and investment risks were buried across handwritten, multilingual, typed, and sketched files.

Research in Action

  • Built the workflow in Python on Google Colab and connected a RAG LLM using Hugging Face tokens.
  • Used Google Sheets for inputs and Google Drive for outputs; used ChatGPT only to help refine the code.

Actionable Insight

  • A chatbot made more sense than asking users to manually inspect every document.
  • Retrieval-augmented generation helped return document-grounded answers for project-specific questions.

Outcome

  • Delivered a working Python-based chatbot POC for project-specific RERA queries.
  • Showed how RAG could reduce effort in legal and builder-reputation checks before investment decisions.

IIIT HYDERABAD · MASTER'S THESIS · VR · QUANTITATIVE RESEARCH

Psycho-motor Correlates of Affective Experience

For my IIIT thesis, I studied whether head-scanning behavior in virtual reality could add an objective layer to emotion assessment.

The Problem

  • Affective experience is subjective, and self-report alone does not always tell the full story.
  • We wanted to see whether head movement in VR could work as a measurable signal of affective response.

Research in Action

  • Led a 6-member master's thesis team at IIIT Hyderabad.
  • Compared yaw-based head-scanning behavior with valence ratings using quantitative correlation analysis.

Actionable Insight

  • Head movement patterns added a behavioral layer to how people experienced VR content.
  • The data helped connect emotion assessment with objective measures, instead of relying only on what users said.

Outcome

  • Found significant positive correlations with valence: yaw SD r(118)=0.223, p=0.015; yaw scanning speed r(118)=0.27, p=0.03.
  • This supported head-scanning behavior as a measurable signal for affective experience in VR.
