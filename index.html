A/B TEST · MIXED-METHOD EVALUATION

Making a complex contact form feel easier

EndNote · VWO · 4 months

01

Problem I solved

A long, single-page contact form created a high-effort first impression. I needed to determine whether splitting it into two focused steps could increase completed submissions.

The primary measure was form completion, with the original inline form as control and a two-step experience as the variation.

02

My approach

I designed and monitored an A/B test, then examined cumulative conversions and conversion rate over four months. Because the experiment reached only moderate statistical confidence, I triangulated the result with session recordings.

The recordings added behavioural context: people completed the multi-step version faster, suggesting that progressive disclosure made the task feel shorter and more manageable.

03

Research outcome and impact

The multi-step form delivered a 12.62% expected improvement in conversion and attracted more completed submissions. Its 66.76% probability of outperforming was not conclusive on its own, so I reported the uncertainty rather than overstating the win.

Combined with faster completion in recordings, the evidence supported the multi-step design as the stronger experience and established a clearer standard for future form optimisation.

BEHAVIOURAL ANALYTICS · BASELINE STUDY

Making high-value content easier to discover

Top 100 Innovators · VWO · Click + scroll data

01

Problem I solved

Traffic to the 2026 listings page fell sharply compared with the previous year. I investigated whether the homepage design and content order were making the route to the list difficult to notice.

The challenge was to distinguish an audience problem from an experience problem and identify where the journey was losing attention.

02

My approach

I combined navigation clicks, heat maps and scroll maps to reconstruct the journey from homepage to list and methodology content. I compared element-level engagement with the points where half of sampled visitors stopped scrolling.

This revealed that the listings pathway appeared too late, while the methodology page strongly matched visitor intent.

03

Research outcome and impact

The analysis linked weaker listings traffic to reduced CTA prominence and showed that roughly half of sampled users dropped before reaching the critical link. It also identified the methodology page as a high-intent destination.

I recommended moving testimonials below key pathways, strengthening the listings CTA, and featuring methodology content more prominently in future campaigns. These findings gave the team a measurable baseline for the next optimisation cycle.
